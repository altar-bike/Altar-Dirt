Soil & Exposure Review

Soil is now Matt's, not mine. Exposure is still every bit a guess — that's what this sheet is for.

Soil: corrected 5 Aug 2026. Seven of the ten spots had been labelled rocky, which carries a wetness multiplier of 0.55 — it halved every rain penalty. Matt's read: Bent Creek, DuPont and Windrock are clay; Pisgah Lower is clay with heavy rock; North Mills and Pisgah Upper are brown dirt, clay and rock together; Hatley Pointe is loam and soft dirt because the trail is still new. Only Wilson Creek is actually rocky. Two in-between grades were added to hold the blends: clayrock and blend.

The cost of that one wrong guess, measured: Bent Creek drops from 87 to about 68 and Pisgah Lower from 69 to about 41 on the same weather. The page had been reading two-to-three condition bands too optimistic on clay after rain.

Exposure is still unverified. Every exposure value below is mine, including mixed for the Pisgah — Upper ridgeline, which is a straight guess about a place I have never been. Exposure multiplies drying by 0.70 / 1.00 / 1.35, so it moves scores about as hard as soil does. This is the column to go after next.

Read this sheet's numbers as a ceiling, not a measurement. The scores in the table are the measured pre-relabel penalties rescaled arithmetically to the new soil classes. The rescale is exact for this hour's penalty but does not re-run the drying curve backwards, and clay sheds water at 0.75 where rocky shed at 1.40 — so the real numbers land somewhat lower still.
